        The actual science (spoiler: over a lifetime, everyone is tier 10)

        A person exhales roughly 7 liters of air a minute. Over a lifetime that's about 7 × 10³⁰ molecules. The atmosphere holds about 10⁴⁴ molecules and mixes globally within a year or two, and nitrogen, 78% of what we exhale, doesn't get used up.

        So every breath you take contains on the order of a billion molecules once exhaled by any given long-lived adult, the President included.

        Today's odds are a different question: could air he exhaled in the last day have reached you? Wind at ground level carries air a few hundred kilometers (a couple hundred miles) a day, so we use his last reported location, today's wind there, and your distance to estimate that. The tiers are for fun. The billion molecules are real.
